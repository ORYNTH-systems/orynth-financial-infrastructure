# Autonomous Economic Agency and Machine Financial Authority

Draft status: WORKING MANUSCRIPT v0.1

Canonical source baseline: financial-suite-v1.0.0

## Abstract

Autonomous financial systems may initiate, optimize, contract, custody, route, or execute consequential financial actions without contemporaneous human intervention. F8 rejects the inference that such capability establishes economic-principal authority.

This paper formalizes autonomous economic agency, machine initiation, delegated machine authority, machine-generated intent, operational autonomy, optimization, principal status, evidence, and governance admissibility. Its controlling separation is autonomous capability != economic principal authority.

The architecture preserves autonomous capability, principal identity, authority source, delegated scope, machine intent, execution evidence, technical validity, and governance admissibility as distinct governed relations.

## 1. Problem

A machine may be technically capable of selecting and executing a financial action while lacking authority to act as an economic principal.

Likewise, autonomous initiation or optimization may demonstrate operational capability without establishing valid principal authorization.

F8 therefore separates autonomous financial capability from economic-principal authority.

## 2. Canonical Boundary

F8 governs autonomous financial capability, machine initiation, machine-generated intent, delegated machine authority, operational autonomy, optimization, principal status, execution evidence, and governance admissibility.

F8 does not permit autonomy, machine intent, optimization success, custody capability, contracting capability, or successful execution to self-create economic-principal authority.

## 3. Autonomous Capability and Economic-Principal Authority

Autonomous capability describes what a machine or automated system can technically select, initiate, or execute without contemporaneous external instruction.

Economic-principal authority determines whether that system is validly authorized to originate or govern the financial consequence.

Autonomous capability != economic principal authority.

## 4. Initiation Capability and Principal Authority

A system may possess the technical ability to initiate a financial transaction, transfer, contract, or other consequential action.

Ability to initiate financial action != authority to act as an economic principal.

Initiation capability must remain separate from the authority basis governing who may originate the consequence.

## 5. Machine-Generated Intent and Authorization

A machine may generate an internal decision, objective, preference representation, or execution choice.

Machine-generated intent != valid principal authorization.

Machine decision formation therefore cannot substitute for a valid authority source.

## 6. Delegated Machine Authority

A machine may receive delegated authority to perform bounded financial actions.

Delegated machine authority cannot exceed the authority and scope validly delegated to it.

Delegation attenuates authority rather than creating a new unbounded principal authority.

## 7. Operational Autonomy and Authority Source

Operational autonomy may allow a system to function without contemporaneous human intervention.

Operational autonomy != independent authority source.

The absence of immediate supervision does not create new governance authority.

## 8. Optimization and Financial Consequence

An autonomous system may optimize for cost, return, timing, liquidity, risk, efficiency, or another objective.

Optimization success != authority for the optimized financial consequence.

Achieving an objective does not establish that the resulting action was authorized.

## 9. Machine Capability and Principal Status

A machine may possess custody, execution, routing, contracting, or programmable financial capabilities inherited from earlier architectural families.

Machine custody, execution, or contracting capability != economic-principal status.

Technical capability cannot silently convert a mechanism into the principal whose interests or authority govern the action.

## 10. Autonomous Evidence and Governance Authority

Evidence may show that a machine selected, initiated, signed, transmitted, optimized, or executed a financial action.

Evidence of autonomous decision or execution != governance authority.

Autonomous execution evidence therefore cannot serve as a self-validating authority source.

## 11. Technical Validity and Governance Admissibility

An autonomous financial action may satisfy its technical execution conditions while failing the authority conditions required for admission.

Technically valid autonomous financial action != governance admission.

Successful execution cannot retroactively establish economic-principal authority.

## 12. Core Non-Equivalence Results

1. Autonomous capability != economic principal authority.
2. Ability to initiate financial action != authority to act as an economic principal.
3. Machine-generated intent != valid principal authorization.
4. Delegated machine authority != authority beyond valid delegated scope.
5. Operational autonomy != independent authority source.
6. Optimization success != authority for the optimized financial consequence.
7. Machine custody, execution, or contracting capability != economic-principal status.
8. Evidence of autonomous decision or execution != governance authority.
9. Technically valid autonomous financial action != governance admission.
10. Successful autonomous financial execution != retroactive economic-principal authorization.

## 13. Runtime and Adversarial Verification

The F8 canonical technical surface tests autonomous capability boundaries, principal-authority separation, delegated scope, machine initiation, machine-generated intent, optimization behavior, technical validity, and adversarial attempts to infer principal authority from autonomous success.

These results are bounded verification evidence for the implemented canonical F8 surface. They are not universal empirical guarantees.

## 14. Cross-Family Interfaces

F8 inherits F5 custody and delegation boundaries, F6 programmable-authority boundaries, and F7 jurisdictional-scope boundaries while preserving the architecture-wide capability / authority separation.

Autonomous systems may compose capabilities from earlier families without acquiring principal, jurisdictional, custody, or programmable authority beyond the valid source and scope governing each action.

## 15. Limits and Non-Claims

This paper does not claim production deployment, regulatory approval, institutional adoption, external certification, customer validation, legal sufficiency, legal personhood, machine rights, or universal prevention of autonomous-finance failure.

The claims are limited to the canonical F8 technical architecture, formal distinctions, runtime behavior, proof surfaces, and adversarial evidence bound in FIN-PUB-37.

## 16. Conclusion

Autonomous financial systems can select and execute consequential actions without thereby becoming valid economic principals.

F8 formalizes the separation among autonomous capability, machine intent, initiation, delegation, optimization, principal status, evidence, admissibility, and authority.

The resulting architecture prevents machine autonomy or execution success from substituting for valid economic-principal authority.
