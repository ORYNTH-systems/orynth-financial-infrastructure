# Cross-Jurisdiction Financial Execution and the Boundary of Technical Reach

Draft status: WORKING MANUSCRIPT v0.1

Canonical source baseline: financial-suite-v1.0.0

## Abstract

Financial infrastructure can execute across networks, borders, counterparties, and technical domains without thereby acquiring authority in every jurisdiction it can reach. F7 rejects that collapse.

This paper formalizes cross-jurisdiction financial execution, jurisdiction-scoped authority, routing, location evidence, authority conflict, and governance admissibility. Its controlling separation is technical reach != jurisdictional authority.

The architecture preserves execution capability, jurisdictional scope, authority source, routing, location, conflict state, admissibility, and consequential authority as distinct governed relations.

## 1. Problem

A financial system may be technically capable of transmitting, routing, signing, settling, or otherwise executing an action across multiple jurisdictions.

That capability does not establish authority to act within every jurisdiction touched by the execution.

F7 therefore separates cross-border technical capability from jurisdictional authority.

## 2. Canonical Boundary

F7 governs technical reach, jurisdiction-scoped authority, cross-border execution capability, jurisdictional conflict, routing, location evidence, and cross-jurisdiction admissibility.

F7 does not permit network reach, territorial reach, routing, location, or successful execution to self-create or expand jurisdictional authority.

## 3. Technical Reach and Jurisdictional Authority

Technical reach describes where a system can transmit, process, route, or execute.

Jurisdictional authority describes whether the action is validly authorized within the applicable jurisdictional scope.

Technical reach != jurisdictional authority.

## 4. Network or Territorial Reach

Ability to execute across a network or territory establishes capability, not jurisdictional authority.

Network or territorial reach != authority to act within that jurisdiction.

Execution capability must remain separately evaluated from the authority basis applicable to the jurisdiction.

## 5. Cross-Border Capability and Authority Expansion

Cross-border capability allows technical execution to span more than one jurisdiction.

Cross-border execution capability != jurisdictional authority expansion.

Technical expansion of reach cannot enlarge authority by implication.

## 6. Jurisdiction-Scoped Authority

Authority may be valid within one jurisdiction while being limited, inapplicable, conflicting, or otherwise non-transferable in another.

Authority valid in one jurisdiction != automatic authority in another jurisdiction.

Jurisdictional scope must therefore remain explicit and independently evaluable.

## 7. Routing and Jurisdiction Source

A transaction may technically pass through infrastructure located in or associated with a jurisdiction.

Technical routing through a jurisdiction != governance authority from that jurisdiction.

Routing describes an execution path rather than an independent authority source.

## 8. Jurisdictional Conflict

Multiple jurisdictions may impose authority conditions that are compatible, partially overlapping, or in conflict.

Jurisdictional conflict must remain representable rather than collapsed into a single successful execution result.

Execution success != resolution of jurisdictional conflict.

## 9. Cross-Jurisdiction Execution and Unresolved Authority

Successful execution under one jurisdictional basis does not resolve incompatible authority conditions in another.

Execution under one valid jurisdictional basis != universal jurisdictional validity.

Each applicable authority condition must remain separately traceable.

## 10. Location Evidence and Jurisdiction

Evidence of geographic, network, infrastructure, account, or system location may inform jurisdictional analysis.

Location evidence != jurisdictional authority.

Observed location alone does not establish the authority source applicable to the financial action.

## 11. Technical Validity and Governance Admissibility

A cross-jurisdiction transaction may satisfy technical execution requirements while failing one or more jurisdictional authority conditions.

Technically valid cross-jurisdiction transaction != governance admission.

Successful execution cannot retroactively establish jurisdictional authority.

## 12. Core Non-Equivalence Results

1. Technical reach != jurisdictional authority.
2. Network or territorial reach != authority to act within that jurisdiction.
3. Cross-border execution capability != jurisdictional authority expansion.
4. Authority valid in one jurisdiction != automatic authority in another jurisdiction.
5. Technical routing through a jurisdiction != governance authority from that jurisdiction.
6. Jurisdictional conflict != a single technical execution result.
7. Execution success under one jurisdictional basis != resolution of incompatible authority elsewhere.
8. Location evidence != jurisdictional authority.
9. Technically valid cross-jurisdiction transaction != governance admission.
10. Successful cross-jurisdiction financial execution != retroactive jurisdictional authorization.

## 13. Runtime and Adversarial Verification

The F7 canonical technical surface tests jurisdiction-scoped authority, technical-reach separation, routing, location evidence, authority conflict, and adversarial attempts to infer jurisdictional authority from successful cross-border execution.

These results are bounded verification evidence for the implemented canonical F7 surface. They are not universal empirical guarantees.

## 14. Cross-Family Interfaces

F7 inherits F5 and F6 capability / authority boundaries while preserving F3 and F4 distinctions concerning finality, evidence, and represented financial state.

F7 further constrains later autonomous financial systems by preventing technical or machine capability from silently expanding jurisdictional scope.

## 15. Limits and Non-Claims

This paper does not claim production deployment, regulatory approval, institutional adoption, external certification, customer validation, legal sufficiency, or universal resolution of jurisdictional conflict.

The claims are limited to the canonical F7 technical architecture, formal distinctions, runtime behavior, proof surfaces, and adversarial evidence bound in FIN-PUB-32.

## 16. Conclusion

Financial systems can cross borders and networks without acquiring authority merely because they can technically reach them.

F7 formalizes the separation among technical reach, jurisdictional scope, routing, location, authority conflict, admissibility, and execution.

The resulting architecture prevents cross-jurisdiction capability or successful execution from substituting for valid jurisdictional authority.
