# FIN-F2-07 — Inconsistent Observation Semantics

## 1. Purpose

Different participants or domains may observe different states without
those observations automatically proving different underlying realities.

Observation must remain distinct from occurrence.

---

## 2. Observation

Define:

Observe(a,q_d,o,v,t)

as actor or domain a reporting value v for observed object o at time t.

Observation != Occurrence.

---

## 3. Observation Set

Define:

O(q_d,o,t)

as the recognized observation set concerning object o.

---

## 4. Observation Consistency

Define:

ObservationConsistent(q_d,o,t)

iff all applicable recognized observations can coexist under the active
observation semantics.

---

## 5. Observation Conflict

Define:

ObservationConflict(q_d,o,t)

iff at least two recognized observations are mutually incompatible under
the applicable semantics.

---

## 6. Inconsistent Observation != Effect Conflict

ObservationConflict(q_d,o,t) !=> EffectConflict(q_d,t)

Different nodes or institutions may disagree because of:

latency
stale state
scope difference
timing difference
missing evidence
different domain boundaries
data corruption
actual underlying state divergence

Reconciliation must determine which case applies.

---

## 7. Staleness

Define:

ObservationStale(obs,t_eval)

where an observation was valid historically but is not current for the
evaluation time.

Stale != False.

---

## 8. Scope Mismatch

Two observations with different scopes may appear inconsistent while
both are valid within their respective scopes.

Therefore reconciliation SHALL evaluate scope before declaring
substantive contradiction.

---

## 9. Temporal Mismatch

Two observations at different times may legitimately differ.

Temporal difference != contradiction.

---

## 10. Evidence Principle

No observation may become authoritative solely because it is:

newest
oldest
most numerous
most repeated
generated by the fastest node
generated by the most powerful participant

unless an explicit governing rule assigns that precedence.
